## 0. What This Document Is

MDPAF is a method of thought for **gating, analyzing, refining, and extending** an object (product / project / work / feature / idea).

This document contains only the methodology itself — its four verbs, their order, and the beliefs that underpin them. **No execution craft**: no templates, no scoring sheets, no output formats, no operating procedures. Those are derivatives, not thought.

**The whole method in one sentence**: Test truth with "has anyone paid a price for this" (two-pass gatekeeping; the Fine Gate runs the full Seven Pain Questions: real, scale, solution, will, degree, coping), place the idea back into the long chain of the user's world to locate it (Map to build coordinates → Re-slice to fix scope), then **mount value judgments onto their proper scopes** (gaps, incumbents, position, money — each on its own layer), close with "evidenced conclusions + ranked, priced actions," and grow along the chain in four directions while re-forging every candidate — trusting cost, not slogans, throughout, and staying ready to redraw the map at any time.

**Why "multi-dimensional"**: Not just one yardstick (multiple sets of value judgments), and not just one layer of scope (macro/micro layering) — the layers and the yardsticks combined give the full picture.

## 1. The Four Verbs

The body of the methodology is four verbs: **Gate → Analyze → Refine → Extend**. They form a loop: every new object generated by Extend goes back to Gate and runs again.

Unified statement: all four verbs are **actions taken on scope** —

| Verb | What it does to scope |
|---|---|
| **Gate** | Gatekeeps the coarsest scope (the block the idea claims to cover) |
| **Analyze** | Map to build coordinates → Re-slice to fix scope → mount judgments per scope |
| **Refine** | Closes with actions over all known scope |
| **Extend** | Generates new scope candidates and throws them back to Gate |

### 1.1 Gate — "Is it worth it?" (First gatekeeping pass: the Coarse Gate)

Before investing any analysis resources, probe authenticity with one extremely cheap question:

> **Has anyone already paid a price for this?** (Time, money, hassle — all count.)

- Only people who have hurt will pay money, spend time, or endure hassle. For something no one has hurt over, however refined the analysis, you are decorating a hallucination.
- This is the goalkeeper of the whole method: judged "not real," terminate on the spot, cost zero.
- Verbal wanting and polite compliments are not paying a price — only real, incurred cost-behavior counts.
- This verb runs the **Coarse Gate** (first pass): the object is the pain block the initiator **claims**; the purpose is to filter obvious hallucinations at zero cost. After Mapping and Re-slicing, the scope may shift — then the **Fine Gate** runs (second pass, see 1.2 step 2), which is the **full Seven Pain Questions check**.

### 1.2 Analyze — "Map → Re-slice → Measure"

Analysis is no longer a parade of yardstakes taking the stage in turn. **Block-cutting draws the scope; yardsticks judge value on the scope** — so there is no question of "when the yardsticks enter," only "which scope, which set of value judgments." Three steps:

#### Step 1: Map — build the coordinate system (locate in the user's world)

- **The idea is not the top of the scope.** Your project / idea is almost always a block in the middle of a long chain — between macro and micro, embedded in one long chain of the user's world. Taking "the idea as a whole" as the origin means drawing the map with the initiator's own boundaries — a disguised form of **drawing the denominator around yourself**: the denominator says "user perspective," but what is actually drawn is you.
- Operation: place the idea into the long chain it lives in (the complete process by which the user gets this thing done), and **scan outward — before, after, and sideways** — to draw the chain's full shape: the blocks before you (what the user does before reaching you), the blocks after you (what they do after), parallel blocks (branches running alongside). This step only draws; it does not judge or choose.
- **Mapping stopping point (task anchor)**: stop at "the nearest user-task closed loop in which the object's position is clearly visible" (a meal is finished, a trip is completed — the completion signal of a user task). **Anchors are reused across layers**: stage anchors (food hits the table, chopsticks go down) cut blocks; task anchors set the boundary of the long chain — anchors are the universal graduation of the whole scope axis; do not trace upward without limit.

#### Step 2: Re-slice — settle the scope (Side-by-Side Reveal + Coverage Correction + Fine Gate)

- Under the chain's coordinate system, re-examine the cut: which blocks of the long chain does your idea actually cover? Cut along **the user's sense of stages** and settle with the **Split–Merge Double Test** (a block that can swap its neighbors and remains stable in other valid combinations = an independent block; adjacent blocks where replacing any one segment makes it fall apart = strongly coupled = merge into one large block, and only split block-by-block inside the sub-chain — both tests presuppose a declared closed-loop goal; tendency-based criteria, gray zones go back to the stop-splitting rule).
- **Side-by-Side Reveal (anti-self-persuasion)**: write out, side by side, "the blocks you claimed to cover before Mapping" and "the blocks you actually cover after Re-slicing." When the two differ, all subsequent analysis must turn to the **new blocks** — no carrying over the old pain-point description. The effort already spent drawing the chain is not a reason: sunk cost will push you to argue "it's actually still my original block"; the Reveal exists to block exactly that move.
- **Coverage Correction**: the direction and magnitude of the scope shift is itself the first output of this analysis — it is often exactly where the product's positioning truly should be adjusted. Record it honestly.
- **Fine Gate (second gatekeeping pass, full Seven Pain Questions)**: once the scope is settled, run the seven questions over **the blocks actually covered** — ① **Real**: has the user paid a price for them (the original Fine Gate question); ②③ **Scale**: how painful × how many people in pain (intensity × breadth = the total account of pain); ④ **Solution**: is there a solution (if none, ask whether it can be solved; if there is, register the incumbents one by one); ⑤ **Will**: does the user want it solved (has the user taken **escape actions** to get rid of it — you being anxious on their behalf doesn't count); ⑥ **Degree**: how much do existing solutions solve (percentage solved + Weight of Concession + Bonus Hard Constraint, see Incumbents in 1.2 step 3; the percentage has a shelf life — how long it holds under current technical capability, see 2.3 corollary 3); ⑦ **Coping**: after the pain, what then (the Folk Workaround — the real starting line). After the seven questions, **add the Timing Question: "Why now?"** — current technical capability has just barely reached it = the window just opened (the moment the Ambush Position pays off; first-mover advantage is greatest); capability reached long ago yet no one has done it = go back and re-examine ④ (Graveyard) and ⑤ (Will) — "nobody did it" means either a reason exists or corpses do; both must be asked out loud. Evidence cited at the Coarse Gate that belongs to old blocks, if the new blocks differ, is not inherited automatically — new evidence must be supplied. Coarse Gate + Fine Gate = two-pass gatekeeping: first save cost (filter hallucinations), then keep coordinates true (the seven questions aimed at the new blocks).

#### Step 3: Measure — mount judgments per scope

There is no "four yardsticks entering in turn"; what exists is **scope determines the judgment set**:

| Scope | Value judgments mounted |
|---|---|
| **The long chain** (the coordinate system from Mapping) | Ceiling magnitude (long chain / short chain); drift signals: which blocks are growing or shrinking under technology, habits, policy |
| **The block group the object covers** (settled by Re-slicing) | Fine Gate (re-verify paid cost); entry value (how many heavy blocks, choke-point status, broken links); Payment Triangle (object-level roles); whole-board business |
| **Core blocks** (heavy blocks and choke points; light blocks get less) | Incumbents and the Folk Workaround (who is coping on this block), the user's will (do they want it solved), weight (time / difficulty / repetition / cost / span), satisfaction and switching cost, Weight of Concession and Bonus Hard Constraint, Payment Trigger, Opportunity Formula (block-level, see below), zoom-in observation (sub-chains and sub-chain choke points) |
| **Candidate blocks** (generated by Extend) | Full re-forge check: candidates get no exemption — run the full "Map → Re-slice → Measure" process |

- **Mounting density control**: mount fully on heavy blocks and choke points, lightly on light blocks — isomorphic to the stop-splitting rule: layers the question doesn't ask don't get split; blocks the question doesn't ask about don't get mounted.
- **The Opportunity Formula sinks to block level**: opportunity = low satisfaction with existing solutions on **this block**, plus low cost to switch to yours. Opportunity is no longer one chain-level verdict but a block-by-block local judgment — on the same chain, some blocks are opportunities and some are also-rans; the blanket phrase "there's opportunity" hides precisely this.

The key points of each judgment set (mounted on their respective scopes):

**Gap (object level)** — split "wants solved / can solve / actually solves" into three segments: the "wants vs can" gap = a design problem; the "can vs actually" gap = a landing problem; the two diseases need different medicine. "Wants solved" is quoted verbatim; "actually solves" requires measured evidence, or is honestly flagged as absent.

**Incumbents (block level)** — who is already solving on this block? The Folk Workaround (how the user is coping right now) is the real competitor. Satisfaction splits into **Grudging Use** (something ties them; don't touch) versus **complain-and-leave** (opportunity). Switching cost must be unpacked (money, data migration, habit, relationships, relearning). **The user's will**: does the user want to escape — the criterion is escape actions (looked for alternatives, tried tools, spent money); you being anxious on their behalf doesn't count. **Weight of Concession**: for the parts existing solutions don't reach, how does the user concede (endure, detour, improvise, give up) — the lighter the concession, the less the remaining pain is worth; the heavier the concession, the bigger the entry point; the Weight of Concession is the yardstick of the remaining market. **Bonus Hard Constraint**: the extra benefit of "and while solving the original pain, it also…" counts only if someone has **separately** paid a price for that bonus; otherwise record but don't evaluate — never let "it does that too" become the all-purpose excuse. Solutions run in two directions — no solution, ask "can it be solved" (cannot = a science problem, not a product problem); a solution exists, ask **replication difficulty** (easy = anyone can enter; hard = breakthrough qualification or out). The **Graveyard is a warning, not blue ocean**. **Copying is bidirectional**: how easily others can be copied by you (replication difficulty), and how easily you can be copied by others (moat) — check both sides.

**Money (object-level triangle + block-level triggers)** — user, decision-maker, and payer, each named; when they don't coincide, aim every action at the one paying (the pitch is spoken to whoever pays the bill); each block has its own peak of pain, and the charging point sticks to the trigger.

### 1.3 Refine — "What next?"

After the analysis, close into two things, under two iron rules:

1. **Conclusions may not introduce new evidence.** At closing, only facts established earlier may be used — to prevent last-minute storytelling and conclusions escaping beyond the evidence.
2. **Actions must be ranked and priced.** No "strengthen," no "optimize" — wish-verbs that cost nothing and cannot be acceptance-tested. Every action must answer: "which first, what does it cost, what counts as done."

One counter-intuitive answer is permitted: **the best refinement is not refining.** For some objects the conclusion is "stop," and the first line of the action list is "stop investing — cost zero."

### 1.4 Extend — "What else can grow on the chain?"

After position is settled (settled in the coordinate system built by Mapping), scan for growth candidates in four directions along the chain:

| Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Upstream** | Occupy the block ahead of you (the distribution player moves on creation) |
| **Downstream** | Occupy the block behind you (the transaction player moves on payments) |
| **Parallel** | Grow a branch sharing the same resources (drivers and algorithms are reusable, so grow designated driving) |
| **Morph** | Take no new position; reshape the chain itself: split, merge, add, delete |

Growth must pass three gates, none optional:

1. **Re-forge Gate**: every new candidate gets no exemption — returns to "Gate → Analyze" and is fully re-audited from the start (Mapping, Re-slicing, Fine Gate, block-by-block judgment, all of it). Generating candidates costs zero, which is precisely what invites hallucination.
2. **Adjacency Gate**: grow only on parcels that share "users / capability / resources" with the current position. Adjacent on the chain ≠ adjacent in capability — near is not the same as doable.
3. **Priority Gate**: all else equal, occupy choke points first, heavy blocks next, light blocks last.

**Principle: growth is bold; analysis kills.**

### 1.5 Appendix · Incorporation of the Pain Point Analysis Method

The independent method "Pain Point Analysis" (a seven-question funnel: Is it really painful? / How painful? / How many are in pain? / Is there a solution? / Does it need solving? / How much is solved? / After the pain, what then?) is incorporated into this method, mounted as follows:

| Question | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| ① Is it really painful? | Coarse Gate (as-is) + first Fine Gate question |
| ②③ How painful × how many | Fine Gate "scale"; intensity × breadth = the total account of pain |
| ④ Is there a solution? | Fine Gate "solution" → registration starting point for Incumbents |
| ⑤ Does it need solving? | Fine Gate "will" → new Incumbent dial, "the user's will" |
| ⑥ How much is solved? | Fine Gate "degree" → new dials "Weight of Concession," "Bonus Hard Constraint" |
| ⑦ After the pain, what then? | Fine Gate "coping" → Incumbents "Folk Workaround" (pre-existing, now explicit as the real starting line) |

Pain Point Analysis handles the single-point quick screen of "is this pain worth taking on"; this method handles the full process after taking it on. The two share the same criterion of truth (cost is the only lie detector).

## 2. The Three Beliefs That Run Through Everything

Three beliefs support all four verbs. The verbs are the technique; the beliefs are the soul.

### 2.1 Order Is Logic

The order Gate → Analyze → Refine → Extend cannot be swapped:

- If the pain is not real, talking about competition is meaningless;
- If the position is absent, talking about positioning is meaningless;
- If the position is wrong, talking about charging money is meaningless.

Every later question stands on the one before it passing. All answers obtained by skipping order stand on an unverified foundation. The grain of the scope layers is also in the order: Coarse Gate before Mapping, Fine Gate after Re-slicing, yardsticks after the scope is settled — all answers obtained by skipping layers equally have no foundation.

### 2.2 Cost Is the Only Lie Detector

Slogans don't count. Likes don't count. Sentiment doesn't count — only **behavior that has paid a price** counts.

The sharpest judgments in this method all come from here: the Folk Workaround is the real competitor (the user has already paid habit for it); Grudging Use is not an opportunity (the cost of enduring is lower than the cost of leaving); the payment moment is the moment the pain erupts (that is when they are most willing to pay); stage boundaries follow anchors, not durations (the user paid cognition for their own segmentation); the user's will is judged by escape actions, not words (action has cost); the Weight of Concession measures the cost the user is already paying; both gatekeeping passes are cost tests (has anyone paid a price?). Every doubt in an analysis comes down to one question: **what has anyone spent on this?**

### 2.3 Maps Expire

Blocks on the chain change size under technology, habits, and policy — a block may be flattened by a new tool, with the value not disappearing but migrating to adjacent blocks (after payments were flattened, value flowed to data and credit). Under recursion, drift can happen at any layer: an upper block flattens and the sub-chain reorganizes; block replacement is itself the aggregate surface of drift (when "cooking at home" is replaced by "ordering delivery," the structural anchor "eating" is unchanged).

**Drift also happens to solutions**: new solutions emerge as technical capability rises, and old solutions strengthen — "no solution" may just mean the technology hasn't arrived yet (a matter of time, not of science); the remaining space of a "70% solved" problem can be eaten directly by a competitor's technical progress. The criterion is restricted to **the natural extension of existing technology at its current rate of improvement** — no science-fiction leaps are predicted. It follows that **opportunity itself has a shelf life** — the space measured at verdict time must be labeled with a re-verification date.

Three corollaries:

1. **Every conclusion has a shelf life.** Today's choke point may be flattened tomorrow; evaluation conclusions must be periodically re-verified.
2. **Drift is a signal.** "Which block is growing" is itself the hint of the next growth direction — before opening the extension scan, look at where the chain is bulging.
3. **Opportunity has a shelf life.** Solution-layer drift is isomorphic to technology-layer drift: the remaining pain space measured by the Fine Gate and the Incumbent dials must be labeled "how long it holds under current technical capability, and when to re-check" — an opportunity judgment without a re-verification date is considered unfinished.

## 3. What the Method Applies To

- **Applicable objects**: products, projects, works, features, ideas — anything where the question is "should this be done, is it being done right, should it continue."
- **Innate gaps** (honest declaration; must be separately covered in use): external constraints (regulation, timing windows) and executor capability (the same idea with a different person should yield a different conclusion) are not in this method's foundation; the gatekeeping applicability to **methodology-class objects** (using this method to analyze this method itself) is likewise not established. This is not decorative humility — it is the honest labeling of the method's boundary.

---

## 4. What the Method Does Not Promise

- All public cases of this method come from business history with known outcomes — **after-the-fact induction**; its before-the-fact predictive power has no verified track record.
- The method does not promise correct conclusions; it promises **traceable evidence and acceptance-testable actions** — its value is preventing hallucination, not prophecy.
